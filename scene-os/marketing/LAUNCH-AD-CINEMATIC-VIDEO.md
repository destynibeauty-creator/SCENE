## Shot list (5 generated clips + 1 overlay-only pass)

Most video models cap a single generation around 5–10s and can't hold
two different locations in one clip cleanly — so the 15 seconds splits
into 5 short generations, cut together, plus text/logo added in post
(see **Text & logo — build in post**, below, for why).

### SHOT 1 — "The GO Light" · timeline 0:00–0:04, trim to 0:00–0:02
No identity reference needed.

```
CAMERA
Macro close-up, static or the slowest possible push-in. Shallow depth of
field. Vertical 9:16. Ultra-realistic, high contrast, cinematic.

SET
Total black. A small acid-lime (#C6FF00) indicator light — the size of a
tally-light bulb — sits just off-center in frame, unlit.

ACTION
Total darkness for the first half-second. The lime light clicks on with
a sharp, decisive snap — no fade-in. It holds steady, throwing a small
cone of lime light into the black. A thin wisp of fog drifts through the
light beam.

REALISM
Physically accurate light falloff into true black. Visible fog/dust
catching the beam. No lens flare cliché, no bloom overload — a real
practical light, not a glow effect.

LENGTH
4 seconds generated (use the first 2 seconds; the light click should
land in the first half-second).
```

### SHOT 2 — "Whip Into The Studio" · timeline 0:02–0:04
No identity reference needed (empty set).

```
CAMERA
Fast whip-pan / whip-dolly, motivated and smooth — not chaotic. Vertical
9:16. Shallow depth of field with realistic lens blur on foreground
objects as the camera passes them.

SET
A deep black studio. Glossy, reflective black floor holding mirror
reflections of every light. Chrome film equipment (a camera rig on a
slider, a boom arm, a C-stand with a flag) catches hard cyan light from
frame-left and hard magenta light from frame-right as the camera passes.
Subtle fog low to the floor.

ACTION
The camera whips through the gear and settles on a giant black director's
monitor mounted center-room. As the camera arrives, the monitor powers
on — a sharp flicker, then a clean glow, lighting the room's center in
cold white-blue.

REALISM
Real chrome reflections (cyan and magenta bouncing off the metal, not
flat color fill). Believable motion blur on the whip, sharp resolve the
instant it lands on the monitor.

LENGTH
2 seconds.
```

## Text & logo — build in post, not in the AI generator

Every AI video model in current use (Sora, Kling, Seedance, Runway,
Veo) renders on-screen text and UI elements unreliably — that's exactly
why the brief's own negative-prompt list bans "random text" and
"unreadable logo." Don't fight the model for this. Add every text/UI
element as a motion-graphics overlay in your edit (CapCut, Premiere,
After Effects, Canva) using the brand kit: Poppins ExtraBold, off-white
`#F2F2F2`-ish text, chrome for the logo lockup, cyan/magenta corner
brackets (see `BRAND-BIBLE.md`).

**Overlay timing:**

| Time | Element | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:02 | `NOT RANDOM AI.` | Over Shot 1, appears as the light clicks on |
| 0:02–0:04 | `A PRODUCTION SYSTEM.` | Over Shot 2 |
| 0:04–0:07 | `THE FACE MATCHES.` | Over Shot 3 — also add camera-UI graphic overlay: `FPS 24.000`, red `● REC` dot, corner frame guides, a focus reticle, timecode, `WB 3200K` |
| 0:07–0:09 | `THE WORLD MATCHES.` | Over Shot 4 |
| 0:09–0:11 | laptop insert (see below) | Character-profile UI, built entirely as a graphic |
| 0:11–0:13 | `ONE CHARACTER. / A WHOLE WORLD.` | Over Shot 5 |
| 0:13–0:15 | `THE SCENE AI` → `CREATE CINEMATIC CONTENT. / BUILD YOUR DIGITAL WORLD.` → `ACTION.` | Over/after Shot 6, then a clean black end-frame with cyan + magenta corner brackets |

**Laptop insert (0:09–0:11):** shoot or generate a plain establishing
b-roll — a laptop on a dark surface, screen glowing, out-of-focus studio
behind it (no identity reference needed, no readable content on the real
screen). Then composite this UI as a graphic straight onto the screen:

```
CHARACTER: LEAD 01
LOCKED
FACE   ✓
HAIR   ✓
BODY   ✓
STYLE  ✓
WORLD  ✓
```

Add a quick glitch-style scanline sweep across the graphic as a
transition effect — built in the edit, not the AI generation.

**Approved on-screen lines — use only these, exactly as written, no
others:** `NOT RANDOM AI.` / `A PRODUCTION SYSTEM.` / `THE FACE MATCHES.`
/ `THE WORLD MATCHES.` / `EVERY DETAIL LOCKED.` / `ONE CHARACTER. A WHOLE
WORLD.` / `THE SCENE AI` / `CREATE CINEMATIC CONTENT. BUILD YOUR DIGITAL
WORLD.` / `ACTION.`

*(Note: the brief's scene breakdown lists `EVERY DETAIL LOCKED.` for a
"laptop close-up" beat at 0:09–0:11 that isn't in the final 6-shot
timeline above, since the laptop graphic there reads as a checklist, not
a caption. Use `EVERY DETAIL LOCKED.` as that graphic's headline instead
of a separate caption line — it keeps all nine approved lines in the
spot without adding a beat.)*

## Audio direction

No AI video tool reliably generates usable ad audio yet — score this in
post with library/stock SFX:

- **0:00–0:02** — one low, decisive cinematic bass hit, synced to the
  light click.
- **0:02–0:04** — a fast whoosh/riser under the whip-pan.
- **0:04–0:09** — a soft, cold synth pad under the monitor + talent
  shots; a subtle mechanical "click" or shutter sound on the UI overlay
  appearing.
- **0:09–0:11** — a quick digital "scan" / glitch SFX under the
  checklist graphic.
- **0:11–0:13** — the pad swells and opens up as the world expands
  (city ambience — distant traffic, wet-street hiss — fades in under it).
- **0:13–0:15** — a final low hit on the logo card; then a clean cut to
  silence on the black end-frame.

## Tool notes

- **Shots 1–2** (no identity, environment only) run fine on any
  text-to-video model — cheapest place to iterate on the "expensive
  studio" look before spending identity-lock credits.
- **Shots 3, 4, 6** (identity-locked, single location) — use whichever
  tool your MASTER reference already tests well in (per the Creator OS:
  Seedance for lip-sync-critical clips, Nano Banana Pro for stills feeding
  a start-frame). These are silent/no-dialogue, so any identity-lock
  video model works.
- **Shot 5** (the portal transition) is the one shot worth over-generating
  — run it 3–5 times and pick the take where the identity holds cleanest
  through the transition. If your tool supports it, generating it as a
  start-frame → end-frame morph (Shot 3's last frame → a still of her in
  the city) will hold identity far more reliably than a single free-form
  prompt.
- Budget ~2 spoken-word-equivalent seconds per beat if you ever add a
  voiceover line instead of pure text overlays — this spot as written is
  silent/text-only by design, matching the brief.
