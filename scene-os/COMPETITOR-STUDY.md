## Competitor 1 — Yurii Yeltsov · "Viral AI Stories"

**Who:** AI artist/animator (@yurii_yeltsov, ~465K Instagram followers),
15+ years in 3D/animation. Sells the "Viral AI Stories" course at
course.yeltsov.com; his other AI video course lists around the ~$200 tier.

**What he's doing:**
- Cinematic, movie-quality viral AI videos (often with celebrities/public
  figures) — the "how did they make that" effect.
- Compact paid course: 5 video lessons + text versions + a private
  Telegram community. Application-style checkout ("if accepted…") that
  makes it feel exclusive.
- Positions everything as "the exact workflow I use."

**His strengths:** massive visual credibility; small, finishable course;
community attached; scarcity framing.

**His weaknesses vs you:**
- Artist-brain, not creator-brain: he teaches VIDEOS, not a repeatable
  CHARACTER + SERIES system. No continuity system, no comment/keyword
  monetization layer, no niche packs.
- Celebrity-deepfake-adjacent content is a brand-risk lane most creators
  can't and shouldn't follow.

**How SCENE beats him:** you sell a complete operating system (character →
story → continuity → post → monetize), written so a 6th grader can run it.
Say it plainly: *"He makes viral videos. SCENE builds a character your
audience follows like a TV show — and a funnel that pays you for it."*

## Competitor 2 — The "AI Baddie" prompt-pack sellers

**Who:** a wave of sellers on TikTok/Lemon8/Etsy/Stan pushing cheap prompt
packs and ebooks — e.g. "Designer Baddie Collection — 20 AI Image Prompts"
(thedigitallineup.com), "Baddie Prompts" ebooks, "AI Baddies Generators."
Typically $10–$40 digital products aimed heavily at women creators —
luxury/baddie aesthetic, your exact audience.

**What they're doing:**
- Scroll-stopping baddie IMAGES (Midjourney/Higgsfield/Kling prompts),
  luxury looks, "even if you're a beginner" framing.
- Fast TikTok demand capture: "making a baddie AI" is a live search trend;
  they post the result, sell the prompts in bio.
- Volume game: low price, no system, no support.

**Their strengths:** cheap, impulse-buyable, perfectly aimed at the
aesthetic your audience wants; they harvest the exact search terms
("AI baddie prompt") your buyers type.

**Their weaknesses vs you:**
- Images, not episodes. One pretty picture, then nothing — no identity
  lock, no story, no series, no monetization. Buyers post once and stall.
- Zero cultural accuracy care. Your Black/BIPOC-centered hair + outfit
  packs with identity/texture protection are a real moat here — this is
  the most underserved part of the exact market they're selling to.

**How SCENE beats them:** name the gap out loud in your content:
*"A prompt pack gives you a pretty girl. SCENE gives you a character with
a face that never changes, a story people follow, and a comment section
that sells for you."* Price above them, and use their trend traffic:
post your character as the hook, then "Comment [YOUR KEYWORD]."

## Competitor 3 — The AI-influencer builder gurus (keyword-funnel creators)

**Who:** creators running the "I built an AI girl, she makes money, comment
X and I'll show you" playbook. Live examples surfaced: @lilyrose.ai
building "Zara" with a **"Comment READY"** funnel; creators running
**"Comment AI"** and **"AI26"** keyword funnels into course details; and
the creator behind **Jezabel** — an AI influencer at ~1M followers doing
mukbangs, luxury trips and affiliate bags, built and monetized by a Black
woman creator.

**What they're doing:**
- Proof-first hooks: show the AI character's numbers/income, then the
  keyword CTA. The funnel IS the content.
- Monetization stories: affiliate links, brand deals, "she posts while I
  sleep."
- Fast, casual teaching — usually a mini-course or 1:1 upsell behind the
  keyword.

**Their strengths:** the strongest funnels in the niche (exactly the
keyword system we built into your Bonus 03); income-proof storytelling;
posting velocity.

**Their weaknesses vs you:**
- Results-on-screen, no system behind the paywall: buyers report scattered
  lessons, no continuity method, characters that drift, no story
  structure. Refund-risk products.
- Almost all avoid the hardest problem — identity lock across a series —
  which is SCENE's Module 01/02/05 core.

**How SCENE beats them:** match their funnel mechanics (you now have the
keyword system + pinned comments built in), but sell the depth they can't:
*"Anybody can generate her once. SCENE keeps her the SAME in every episode
— that's what makes people follow."*

## The 5 moves to steal (do these now)

1. **Proof-first hooks.** Every promo post opens with the character's
   results (views, followers, comments demanding Part 2) before any
   teaching. All three competitors do this; it works.
2. **One keyword, everywhere.** Pick YOUR word (like their READY / AI26),
   run it in every caption, pin, and reply — your Bonus 03 is now the
   playbook for this; use it on your own account first.
3. **A community layer.** Yeltsov's Telegram is half his retention. Attach
   a private group (Telegram/Discord/IG broadcast) to SCENE.
4. **Application/scarcity checkout.** "If accepted, you'll get the link"
   noticeably lifts perceived value at Yeltsov's tier.
5. **Ride their search traffic.** Post into the live trends ("AI baddie,"
   "making a baddie AI," "AI influencer") with a character that never
   drifts — then let the keyword funnel catch the demand.

## The 3 gaps only SCENE fills (say these in your marketing)

1. **Continuity = series = followers.** Nobody else teaches identity lock
   + world continuity as a system. One-off pretty girls don't build fans;
   episodes do.
2. **Culturally accurate by design.** Black/BIPOC-centered hair, skin and
   outfit systems with texture and identity protection — the baddie market
   is enormous and nobody serving it teaches this properly.
3. **Simple enough to actually finish.** Their buyers get scattered
   lessons; yours get a road map, part numbers, copy-paste panels and a
   6th-grade reading level. "Easiest AI system you've ever used" is a
   true claim — keep making it.
